## A. Fair Labor Standards Act — Minimum wage · 29 U.S.C. § 206

- **Official:** https://uscode.house.gov/ (Title 29 § 206). Search result text from uscode.house.gov stated laws in effect on **September 1, 2026**.
- **DOL plain-language confirmation:** https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/wages/minimumwage — federal minimum wage for covered nonexempt employees is **$7.25 per hour**.

### § 206(a)(1) (excerpt) 【Verified · uscode.house.gov search text 2026-11-02】

Every employer shall pay to each of his employees who in any workweek is engaged in commerce or in the production of goods for commerce, or is employed in an enterprise engaged in commerce or in the production of goods for commerce, wages at the following rates:

(1) except as otherwise provided in this section, not less than—

(A) $5.85 an hour, beginning on the 60th day after May 25, 2007;

(B) $6.55 an hour, beginning 12 months after that 60th day; and

(C) **$7.25 an hour**, beginning 24 months after that 60th day;

> Practical rule: covered nonexempt employees generally must receive at least **$7.25/hour** federally. If state/local MW is higher, pay the higher rate (DOL WHD).

## B. Fair Labor Standards Act — Overtime · 29 U.S.C. § 207(a)(1)

- **Official cite:** 29 U.S.C. § 207 — https://uscode.house.gov/  
- **Text retrieval note:** Direct house.gov page fetch returned temporary “Page Not Found” on 2026-11-02; operative overtime rule cross-checked via Cornell LII mirror of U.S. Code text and longstanding DOL WHD publications. Treat full subsection quotes as **[TO VERIFY]** against uscode.house.gov if house.gov search is restored; the **40-hour / 1.5×** rule below is the standard FLSA overtime baseline used by DOL.

### Core rule (summary; cite 29 U.S.C. § 207(a)(1)) 【Rule verified via DOL practice · full USC quote TO VERIFY on house.gov】

Except as otherwise provided, no employer shall employ any covered employee for a workweek longer than **forty hours** unless the employee receives compensation for employment in excess of forty hours at a rate not less than **one and one-half times** the regular rate at which the employee is employed.

## F. California overtime (state) — high-level 【Partial · cite Labor Code / IWC Orders】

California generally requires overtime for many nonexempt employees at:

- **1.5×** after **8 hours** in a workday or after **40** in a workweek, and for first 8 hours on the **seventh** consecutive day in a workweek;  
- **2×** after **12** hours in a workday and for hours beyond 8 on the seventh consecutive day.

Exact text and exemptions: Cal. Lab. Code § 510 and applicable IWC Wage Orders — **[TO VERIFY exact current Lab. Code § 510 wording on LegInfo before quoting verbatim]**. Never tell a CA worker that “only weekly overtime exists.”

## H. At-will employment (baseline)

Most US states follow employment **at-will** as a default: either party may end the relationship for any lawful reason or no reason, subject to contract, statute, and public-policy exceptions. California is an at-will state by statute (Lab. Code § 2922) **[TO VERIFY exact § 2922 text on LegInfo]** with significant statutory protections layered on top.

## I. Enforcement pathways (overview)

1. Internal complaint / written wage demand  
2. State labor agency (e.g., CA DLSE; NY DOL; FL DBPR/related; IL IDOL; TX may emphasize federal WHD for FLSA)  
3. USDOL Wage and Hour Division (FLSA)  
4. Private lawsuit / collective or class where allowed  
5. EEOC / state FEP agency for discrimination
